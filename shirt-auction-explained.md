# Running the shirt auctions on our own website

**A proposal for the board**

---

## What it would look like to a supporter

They see the shirt on our website. To bid, they put in their email address and we send them a link — the same way members already sign in. One tap and they're in. No password to remember.

They see the current bid and what the next one has to be. They put a number in. Their bid appears straight away with their first name and the time.

If somebody outbids them, they get an email telling them. They can come back and bid again.

When it closes, the winner gets an email with a payment link. They pay through SumUp by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay — exactly as they would when joining the Trust, and the money lands in the same account.

---

## How we know the bids are real

**Everyone who bids has proved they own an email address.** That's the important part. Nobody can type in a name and a number without it — the link has to be clicked before a bid counts.

**A bid cannot be taken back.** Once it's in, it's in, and it's recorded with the exact time from our server rather than whatever time is on somebody's phone.

**Every bid is logged permanently.** Who, how much, when. If anybody ever queries the result, we can show the whole thing.

**There are minimum steps.** You can't bid a penny more than the last person. We'd set it at something sensible — £5 on a shirt, say.

**The board is emailed every time a bid is placed** — who bid, how much, and when. So it is being watched as it happens, not just checked at the end.

---

## The sniping problem, and how we stop it

Wherever an auction runs to a fixed time, people wait until the final seconds and drop a bid in. Everyone else misses it, the shirt goes cheap, and it puts people off bidding at all.

**We'd fix that automatically.** There would always be at least five minutes left after the last bid. If somebody bids inside the final five minutes, the clock resets to five minutes from that bid, and it keeps going until nobody has bid for five clear minutes.

The result is people bid what the shirt is actually worth to them, instead of waiting.

---

## What happens if the winner doesn't pay

This is the one real risk, and it applies wherever we run an auction.

**Our answer:** the winner gets 48 hours. If they haven't paid, it goes to the next highest bidder, who gets the same 48 hours. Everyone knows this before they bid because it's written on the page.

We can't take payment upfront without holding people's card details, which brings rules we don't want to be responsible for.

---

## What running it ourselves would give us

- **Everyone can see the full bid history**, not just the last comment
- **Bidders get told when they're outbid**, so they come back
- **No lost bids** in a thread that's hard to follow
- **A proper record** of what each shirt raised, for the charity and for us
- **It works for exiles** — anyone, anywhere, in any time zone
- **Open to everyone**, member or not, whether or not they use social media
- **The board sees every bid as it happens**

---

## What we'd need to agree

1. **The minimum bid step** — £5 seems right for a shirt
2. **How long a winner gets to pay** — 48 hours is my suggestion
3. **Who chases if they don't**
4. **Whether we start with a reserve** or just let it run

---

## How long it would take

**About 24 hours of work.** Bidding, verifying who people are, the clock, the emails, and a screen for whoever is running it.

It will be tested end to end before the shirt goes live — bidding, the clock, the emails and the payment — with a practice auction that nobody sees.

**The money is for charity**, so it needs to be right first time. Anything that does go wrong is fixable while an auction is running: bids are permanent, the board sees every one as it lands, and the whole history is there if a result is ever questioned.
