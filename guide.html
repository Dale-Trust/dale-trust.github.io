Signing in

  1. Go to daletrust.uk/admin.html
  2. Type your email address — the one the Trust has for you
  3. Press Email me a sign-in link
  4. Check your inbox and tap the link

There is no password. The link works once and lasts two hours, and needs opening on the same device you asked from. After that you stay signed in on that device for about a month.

If the email does not arrive, check your junk folder first — it comes from membership@daletrust.uk and can land there the first time. There is a “not getting the email, or something not right?” link on the sign-in page: use it and someone will sort it out.
You only see the tiles you have been given. If someone else has more than you, that is deliberate rather than broken — ask Chris if you need something.

Who does what

Posts and anything published on the website are Ruby’s — that is the Media and Youth Inclusion role. Membership is Scott’s. Coach travel is Emma’s. Partners and visiting trusts are Keith’s.

That does not mean only they can write. Anyone with access can write a post — it simply waits for Ruby to approve before it goes anywhere. One person approving keeps the tone and the standard consistent, and stops two people posting the same thing.

Membership register

Tile: Membership register · mainly Scott

Adding a member who did not join online

For anyone who joins at the Trust Desk, sends a cheque, pays by standing order, or rings up.

  1. The Add a member form is at the top of the page and always open
  2. Fill in what you know. Only the first name and surname are required
  3. Choose the membership — Adult, Exile or Junior
  4. Choose the season, unless you are recording something historic
  5. Email is optional. If they have not got one, leave it blank — they will be recorded properly and left out of emails automatically
  6. Choose how they paid: Trust Desk / cash, cheque or bank transfer, free (junior or honorary), or not paid yet
  7. Press Add to the register

If someone with that name and postcode is already on the register for that season it warns you and offers Add anyway — because families and namesakes exist.

Anyone marked as paid gets a membership number straight away. Anyone marked not paid yet does not get one until the money arrives.

Send them a welcome email is ticked by default. They get exactly the same welcome as someone joining online — their number, and a link to their own card and welcome letter. Untick it only if they have already been welcomed some other way.

Someone who lapsed and has come back

Do not retype them. Search their name, open the old record, and press Bring them back for this season — the green button only appears on records from an earlier season.

It asks how they paid and checks their email, then creates a fresh record for this season carrying over their address, phone, date of birth and everything else. They get a membership number and the welcome email, and they are recorded as renewing rather than new.

Their old record is left exactly as it was. Nothing is overwritten, so the history stays intact.

When someone leaves

Each record has an If they have left panel. Choose a reason — payment defaulted, standing order cancelled, resigned, moved away, deceased, or asked not to be contacted — and add a note if it helps. This keeps their history and stops them being contacted. It is not the same as deleting.

Deceased is different and permanent. It applies to every season that person appears in, removes them from every mailing and count, and stops any future spreadsheet import bringing them back. Use it, and use nothing else, when someone has died.

What the tags mean

  • details needed (amber) — imported with an email but no name. They can fill it in themselves when they sign in
  • do not contact (red) — they have asked not to be emailed
  • deceased (black) — will never be contacted again
  • bad address — email to them has bounced. They are left out of every mailing until it is corrected

After adding someone, press Clear the form and the next one is ready.

Finding someone

Type into the search box — a name, a postcode, a town, a membership number, an email address or a mobile number all work. Narrow it by season if you like.

Each result shows their number, membership type, season, where they live and whether they have paid. Anyone without an email address is flagged in red.

Correcting a record

Click anyone in the search results and the full record opens underneath. Change what needs changing and press Save changes.

Above the fields it tells you whether they have ever confirmed their own details, and lists their other seasons as links — so you can jump between someone’s 2024/25 and 2026/27 records.

Removing a record

If a member has simply left, do not delete them. Set their payment status to cancelled instead. That keeps their history and their place in the Trust’s records.

Deleting is for genuine mistakes, duplicates, or where someone has asked to be removed entirely. Press Delete this record, say why, type DELETE, and confirm. It cannot be undone, and a note of what was removed is kept permanently.

Membership dashboard

Tile: Membership dashboard · needs Members

Drop Scott’s spreadsheet onto the page and it draws it: membership by type, new against renewing, age profile, where people live, overseas by country, how they joined, and a data health check showing what is missing.

It changes nothing. It is for looking at a spreadsheet before deciding whether to import it, or for answering a question the board figures do not cover.

If you want the same picture from the live register rather than a spreadsheet, use Board figures instead. That one needs no file and is always current.

Membership letters

Tile: Membership letters · needs Members

Prints the card letters, and the membership cards themselves. One wording that changes itself depending on who it is going to.

Printing the letters

  1. Choose the season and who — everyone, adults and exiles only, juniors only, new members, or those who donated
  2. Press Find them to see how many
  3. Preview a letter to check it, then Print the letters

Only members with a membership number are included, so nobody unpaid gets one. Choose Save as PDF in the print box if you want a file rather than paper.

Page two is a cut-out temporary card, on unless you untick it. Useful for exiles and anyone who cannot get to the Trust Desk — they can use it the day the letter arrives.

Printing the cards

Print the cards puts ten to an A4 sheet at proper business-card size, with light cutting guides. Each one carries the member’s name and number, so there is nothing to write by hand.

Use 300gsm card if you have it, and set the printer to actual size rather than fit-to-page, or they come out slightly small. Print one sheet on plain paper first and hold a bank card against it.

For a single replacement card, use the Print their card button on the member’s record in the register instead.

Changing the wording

The letter is one piece of text that adapts itself. Anything between {{#junior}} and {{/junior}} only appears on junior letters, and the same for new, renewal and donation. {{first_name}}, {{member_no}}, {{season}} and {{donation}} fill themselves in.

Every change is kept, so it is always clear what a member was actually sent.

Checking an email before it goes

Send yourself a test shows exactly what a member receives — the welcome, the unpaid reminder, or either of the coach emails. Use a real member’s details or a made-up one. The links in it are live, so you can follow the whole thing through.

Member import

Tile: Member import · needs Import · Chris only

Loads a spreadsheet into the register. Rarely needed now the register is the source of truth, but here for a bulk update from Scott’s sheet.

  1. Drop the file on and pick the tab
  2. Check the column matching — it guesses, and it is usually right
  3. Choose the season
  4. It tells you how many will import, how many have an email but no name, and how many will be skipped

A row needs either a name or an email address. Rows with neither are skipped — usually blank lines or totals. Anyone with an email but no name goes in marked details needed so they can fill the rest in themselves.

Anyone already on the register for that season is skipped, not duplicated. So re-importing the same sheet is safe. Anyone recorded as deceased is never re-added, whatever the spreadsheet says.

Renewals

The go-live email lives at the top of this screen. It is the one that tells every member their membership is now online. It is sent once, it asks twice before going, and it warns you if it has been sent before. Leave it alone unless you know you are meant to be sending it.

Tile: Renewals · Chris and Scott

Shows how many members are due to renew, how many have, and how many are left. There is a progress bar, and a count of members with no email address who will need reaching another way.

Sending the notices

Three go out in order — first notice, reminder, then last call. Send yourself a test first. Anyone already sent a particular notice is skipped automatically, so pressing it twice will not double-email anybody.

Each email carries a personal sign-in link, so the member taps it and lands in their own membership already signed in, with their details filled in and a renew button waiting. That is the whole point — the old form is what cost us hundreds of members.
Renewals are not switched on at the moment, so members see no mention of them. Turn them on in the settings when the time comes and everything else follows.

Rolling the season over

Once the new season has started and renewals are largely done, press Roll the season over. Next season becomes this season, the figures for the season just gone are recorded permanently, and renewals close. Once a year, and it asks you to type a phrase to confirm.

Fixtures & results

Entering a score takes two seconds. Find the game in the list, type the score in the box beside it — like 2-1 — and press Enter. Pressing Edit on a row opens the full details underneath that row, so you never lose your place.

Tile: Fixtures & results

The home page looks after itself — it finds the next game, counts down to kick-off, and rolls on to the following one automatically after the whistle.

The one thing it cannot know is the score. Put it in here after a game and the “last time out” line under the countdown updates itself.

If a score has not been entered yet, the home page says so rather than reaching back to an older game — so a forgotten score looks like a gap, which is your nudge to fill it in.

Writing a post

Tile: Post composer · anyone with access

The Trust does not send long newsletters. You write it once, it goes on the website, and the email is a short note pointing people at it. That way the piece lives at daletrust.uk where anyone can find it later.
  1. Give it a title and pick a category
  2. Write it in the big box:
    • Leave a blank line between paragraphs
    • A line starting with # becomes a heading
    • **words like this** come out bold
    • [the words to show](https://the-web-address) makes a link
    • ![](https://address-of-a-photo.jpg) puts a photograph in
  3. The right-hand side shows exactly how it will look
  4. Press Send it, or switch to Schedule and pick a date and time

If you have approval rights it goes live immediately. If you do not, it queues for approval — and you will get an email when it goes up, or if it comes back for another look.

Approving a post

Ruby, and anyone else given approval rights

Anything waiting appears in the queue on the admin home page and in the article manager. Open it, read it, and either:

  • Approve — it goes on the website and the writer is told
  • Send it back — with a note saying why, which the writer receives

The writer is credited on the piece and the approver is recorded. Nothing reaches the public without someone having read it.

Managing articles

Tile: Manage articles

Every article on the site, newest first, with a status pill. Search across titles and text to find anything — there are over three hundred going back to last summer.

Open one to edit the title, category, date or wording. Save and the website updates immediately.

To take something off the site, set it to Draft or Archive it. Nothing is ever deleted — an archived piece can be set back to published at any time.

Email members without putting it on the website

Tile: Email members · needs Email

For things members should know that do not belong on the website — an AGM notice, coach seats left with a deadline, something for exiles only, a members’ survey.

Anything that is genuinely news should be written as an article instead. That way it lives on the website and people can find it later. This is for notices, which are no use to anyone a week afterwards.

It gets checked before it goes

Anyone who can write may compose one, but only someone who can email members can actually send it. So the tone and the wording get a second pair of eyes before it reaches four hundred inboxes.

If you cannot send, the button says Send it for checking. It goes into a queue, everyone who can email is told, and you are emailed back when it is sent or if it comes back with a note. Nothing reaches a member until someone approves it.

How it works

  1. Write a subject and the message — their first name is added at the top for you
  2. Choose who gets it: membership type, where they live, whether they take the newsletter
  3. It counts them as you go, one per address so a household gets one email rather than three
  4. See how it will look, and send yourself a test
  5. Send it. You are asked twice, because emails cannot be unsent

Everything sent is kept at the bottom of the screen — the subject, how many people, and who sent it.

Emailing members

Most pieces just go on the website. The composer asks “Email this out to members as well?” — leave it unticked unless members really should get it in their inbox. Four hundred emails about a match report is how people stop reading them.

Anyone with sending rights

Once a post is published the email options appear underneath. There are no lists to keep up to date — you describe who you want and it works them out.

  • Who — current members, lapsed members, or a past season
  • Membership type — Adult, Exile, Junior
  • Where — anywhere, the UK, overseas, a county or a country
  • Age — any, under 16, 16 to 39, 40 to 59, 60 or over, or no date of birth recorded
  • Newsletter — opted in only, or everyone with an email

The number updates as you go, describes the audience in words, and tells you how many members have no email address and are being left out.

Always send yourself a test first and read it on your phone. Then Send to [n] people.

Emailing lapsed members asks an extra question about the board’s approval. That is deliberate — say no if you are not sure.

Every send is recorded: what went out, to whom, how many, and who sent it.

Sharing it

Underneath there are ready-written versions for Facebook, X, LinkedIn, Instagram and YouTube, each the right length for that platform. Press Copy text and paste it in. Post it properly on each platform rather than sharing a link from one to another — it reaches far more people.

Surveys & voting

Tile: Surveys & voting

Player of the Month

  1. Press + New Player of the Month vote — the question is already written
  2. In the Choices box type the players’ names, one per line
  3. Set when it closes, who can take part, and when results are shown
  4. Set the status to Open and press Save

Any other survey

Press + New survey and add your own questions. Each can be one choice, several choices, a rating out of five, or a written answer.

Getting people to take part

Once open, an Email it out panel appears with the same audience picker. It also appears on its own on the home page, in the members’ area (where members are told whether they have taken part), and at daletrust.uk/vote.html.

The results

Shows the total and how many came from verified Trust members. Anyone can respond, but a member who is signed in is counted as verified — and “312 responses, 198 from verified members” is a far stronger thing to put in front of the club than a bare number.

Written answers are never published, whatever the setting. Only the counts. People answer honestly when they know their comments are not going on a website.

Coach travel

Tile: Coach travel · Emma

Every away game is already there

You do not need to set coaches up. Every away league game is already listed, with the ground, the real kick-off time, a suggested departure time and a price based on the distance. The next one is always at the top.

Press Edit on any of them and the details open underneath that coach. Change whatever needs changing — the departure time and price are only suggestions — and press Save.

Nothing is on the website until you say so. Each coach has a tick box: Not shown yet until you tick it, then On the website and people can book. Untick it and it comes straight back off.

If you need one that is not in the list — a cup tie, or a second coach — press Set up a new coach at the bottom.

Cancelling someone’s seat

Marking a booking cancelled now emails the passenger to tell them, and asks you why first — the reason goes in the email. If they had paid, it says we will be in touch about the refund. If there is no email address on the booking it warns you, so you know to ring them.

Who is on board

Every booking now carries a name and date of birth for each passenger. That is how the driver knows who is travelling, and how the rule works that anyone aged 15 or under must be with a responsible adult aged 18 or over — a booking without an adult is refused automatically. On the passenger list, anyone under 16 is shown with their age in red.

Taking a booking over the phone

Click the coach, then use Take a booking: name, mobile, how many seats, and how they paid — cash, card, not yet, or a free seat. It goes straight onto the passenger list.

The passenger list

Shows everyone booked with their mobile numbers, seats and payment status, and a running total: seats booked, money taken, anything still owed, seats left. People who paid online are marked as paid automatically.

Anyone who has not paid has a Mark paid button for when they hand it over on the day, and a Cancel button that frees the seat.

Print / save the list gives you a clean passenger list for the morning — no menus, no buttons, just names and numbers.

Graphics maker

Tile: Graphics maker

Pick what you are making — Player of the Month, match result, next fixture, join the Trust, coach travel, an event, or a quote card. Fill in three or four boxes. Choose Square for Facebook and Instagram, Landscape for X, or Story. Press Download the image.

Drop a photograph in and it sits behind the text, darkened so the words stay readable. Everything comes out in Trust colours with the badge and daletrust.uk on it.

Nobody should be starting from a blank page in Canva. Everything the Trust puts out ought to look like it came from the same organisation.

Visiting trusts

Tile: Visiting trusts · Keith

The note we send to a visiting club’s supporters’ trust before they come to Rochdale — free parking at the Cricket Club and Spotland Bridge Mill, where to drink, and a link to our away fans guide.

  1. Pick who is visiting and type the match date
  2. The note fills itself in
  3. Press Copy the note and paste it into an email
  4. Press Mark as sent so we know who has been contacted

Underneath is every club in the division with space for their trust name, contact and email. Press Edit and the fields open underneath that club. Fill them in as you find them, and next season the job is picking a name and pressing copy.

Edit the wording for everyone changes the shared version — so if you improve a line, everyone gets the better one.

Board figures and the dashboard

Tiles: Board figures, Membership dashboard

Board figures is the simple one: members this season by type, new against renewing, how many seasons on record, newsletter consent, and how many lapsed members are there to win back. No personal details on the page at all.

Membership dashboard is the detailed one: season history back to 2012/13, lapsed by season, joins by month, age profile, how people joined, top towns, and the exiles broken down by country and county.

How it’s going

Tile: How it’s going · needs Figures

Whether the site is actually being used, rather than just that it exists. Members signed in, how many have checked their details, coach seats and money taken, survey responses, articles published, emails sent and how many arrived.

It also flags the problems: addresses that bounce, members with no email at all, applications awaiting payment, and anyone who has asked for help.

The two worth watching are signed in and details checked. They start near zero and should climb after members are emailed. If they do not move, people are not finding their way in.

Trust email addresses

Tile: Trust email addresses · everyone

Who has what, and where the shared addresses go. Tap any address to copy it.

Everyone has two: a personal one that is yours, and a role one that belongs to the job. Both arrive in the same inbox.

Reply from the role address when it is Trust business. There is a “From” dropdown when you write an email — pick the role one. That way anything on the website or a poster keeps working when someone else takes the job on, and members see a Trust address rather than a personal one.

The shared addresses — info@, matchday@, board@ and the rest — go to several people, and anyone on them can reply as the address rather than as themselves.

On your phone, download Zoho Mail and sign in. On a computer, go to mail.zoho.eu.

Decisions & tasks

daletrust.uk/decisions.html · all directors

Everything raised about the website in one place. Anything still open is at the top; anything answered is below for the record. Pick your name, type your answer, and it saves for everyone. No need to reply by email.

Policies

Tile: Policies

The Trust’s policies live here — the coach travel code of conduct, the travel policy, privacy, and anything else the Trust is expected to publish. They appear on the site at daletrust.uk/policies.html.

What you write here is the policy. There is no separate document to keep in step with it — this is the version of record. A member reading the code of conduct on their phone at the coach gets the wording, not a download.

Writing or changing one

  1. Press Edit and the fields open underneath that policy
  2. Write it in plain words — blank line between paragraphs, # for a heading, - for a bullet
  3. Give it a version, the date it was agreed, and who agreed it
  4. Say what has changed if you are amending one — it is saved against the version you are replacing
  5. Set it to On the site and save

Nothing is lost

Every time the wording changes, the version being replaced is kept in full. Earlier versions shows the history, Read it shows exactly what it said at the time, and Put this one back restores an earlier version if a change was wrong — keeping the current one in the history as well.

So the Trust can always show what was in force on any date, and who agreed it. That is the whole point of writing policies down.

Linking a policy to a post

In the composer there is an Include a policy? box. Pick one and the article carries a link to it at the bottom — so a post about coach travel points at the code of conduct without anyone having to remember the address.

Activity

Tile: Activity · admins

Everything officers have done, newest first, in plain words — who added a member, who published what, who approved it, who sent an email and to whom, who deleted something and why.

Search by person or by anything in the detail. Anything that removes or changes something significant is flagged in red.

This protects officers as much as it checks them. If a question comes up about who changed what, the answer is there rather than depending on anyone’s memory.

Officers & access

Chris

On the admin home page. Add someone with their name, email and role, then tick what they should have. Each tool has its own switch, so people get exactly what their job needs:

  • Members — the membership register
  • Import — loading the spreadsheet
  • Renewals — the renewal screen and notices
  • Write — writing posts (they still go for approval)
  • Publish — approving posts and putting them live
  • Email — sending to members
  • Surveys — Player of the Month and consultations
  • Coach — coach travel and passenger lists
  • Graphics — the graphics maker
  • Visiting — the visiting trusts note and contacts
  • Policies — writing and changing policies
  • Auctions — setting up a shirt auction, watching the bids and settling it. Separate from Write, because it handles money and charity funds
  • Figures — board figures, no personal details
  • Admin — officer access and the activity log

Ticking a box takes effect immediately, and the change is recorded.

Which switch opens which screen

They are grouped by what a person can see or do, not one per screen — so some cover more than one:

  • Members — the register, the membership dashboard and the letters. This is the one that shows addresses, phone numbers and dates of birth, so it should stay with as few people as possible
  • Renewals — the renewals screen. Separate from Members, because it can email hundreds of people at once
  • Write — articles and fixtures
  • Figures — board figures and How it’s going. No personal details, so most officers can have it
  • Everything else opens one screen each

Decisions & tasks and Trust email addresses are open to every officer and have no switch. Activity is admins only, deliberately — a log people can grant themselves access to is worth rather less.

They sign in with their own email. Nothing to share, nothing to leak, and access can be changed or withdrawn in seconds.

Writing and approving are separate. Give someone articles and they can write; give them publishing as well and they can put things live. That is how anyone can contribute while Ruby still decides what goes out.

Things worth knowing

Nothing can be deleted by accident. Articles are archived rather than removed. Members are cancelled rather than deleted. Anything that genuinely removes something asks you to type a word to confirm and keeps a record of what went.

Everything is recorded. Who added a member, who sent an email and to whom, who published what and who approved it. Not to catch anyone out — so that questions can be answered later.

Members without an email address are never silently dropped. Anywhere an audience is counted, it tells you how many are being left out.

If something looks wrong, it probably is. Tell Chris rather than working around it.

If you get stuck, the worst that happens is nothing happens. There is no button in here that can break the website.

This guide lives inside the admin area rather than as a document, so it is always the current version and there is nothing to lose or be sent. It is the first thing on the admin page.
